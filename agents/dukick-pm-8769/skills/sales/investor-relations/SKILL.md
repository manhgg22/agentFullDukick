# Investor Relations

Research markets, build investor materials, and execute outreach — from first contact to closing.

## When to Activate

- Raising a round (Seed, Series A, etc.)
- Building or updating a pitch deck, one-pager, or investor memo
- Researching competitors, market size, or investor fit
- Drafting cold emails, warm intros, or investor updates
- Preparing a data room for due diligence
- User says "pitch deck", "investor", "fundraising", "data room", "market size", or "competitor analysis"

---

## Phase 1: Market Research

### Market Sizing

Build a defensible TAM/SAM/SOM with bottom-up and top-down triangulation.

**Top-down:**
```
TAM = Total addressable market (industry reports, analyst data)
SAM = Serviceable addressable market (segment you can reach)
SOM = Serviceable obtainable market (realistic 3-5 year capture)
```

**Bottom-up:**
```
SOM = (Target customers) × (Average contract value) × (Expected penetration)
```

Always show both methods and explain discrepancies.

### Competitive Analysis

Build a competitor matrix covering:
- Product/features
- Pricing model
- Target customer
- Strengths/weaknesses
- Key differentiator vs your approach

Sources: Crunchbase, G2, Capterra, company websites, investor updates.

### Investor Database

Track potential investors:
- Firm, partner, stage focus, thesis fit
- Warm intro path (who knows them)
- Portfolio conflicts
- Recent investments in adjacent spaces
- Response status and notes

## Phase 2: Investor Materials

### Pitch Deck Structure

1. **Problem** — specific, painful, widespread
2. **Solution** — your unique approach
3. **Market** — TAM/SAM/SOM with sources
4. **Product** — demo or screenshots
5. **Traction** — metrics, growth, customers
6. **Business Model** — pricing, unit economics
7. **Competition** — landscape + differentiation
8. **Team** — relevant experience
9. **Financials** — 3-5 year projections
10. **Ask** — amount, use of funds, milestones
11. **Appendix** — detailed metrics, tech deep dive

### One-Pager

Single-page summary for warm intros:
- Headline: what you do in one line
- Problem + solution (2-3 sentences each)
- Traction highlight
- Team snapshot
- Ask and contact

### Investor Memo

Detailed narrative for serious conversations:
- Company overview and origin story
- Market analysis with data
- Product deep dive
- Go-to-market strategy
- Financial projections with assumptions
- Risk factors and mitigations
- Use of funds and milestones

### Data Room

Organized folder structure for due diligence:
```
/01_Company_Overview
/02_Financials
/03_Legal
/04_Product
/05_Technical
/06_Market_Research
/07_Team
/08_Customer_References
```

## Phase 3: Investor Outreach

### Cold Emails

**Subject line:** Specific, not generic. "[Company] — [specific claim about their portfolio or market]"

**Structure:**
1. **Hook** — why them specifically (reference their portfolio, a recent tweet, or thesis)
2. **Problem** — 1-2 sentences, concrete
3. **Solution** — your unique approach
4. **Traction** — metric or milestone
5. **Ask** — 15-min call, not a deck review
6. **Social proof** — warm connection, advisor, or customer

**Example:**
> Subject: Resolving the LLM context-length bottleneck — noticed your Series A in [Company]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> [Company] is building [specific solution] for [specific customer]. We [traction metric].
>
> Given your investment in [Portfolio Company], I'd love to get your take on [specific question].
>
> Would you have 15 minutes next week?
>
> Best,
> [Name]

### Warm Intros

1. Ask the connector for a double-opt-in intro
2. Provide the connector with a forwardable blurb (3-4 sentences)
3. Follow up within 24 hours of the intro
4. Thank the connector regardless of outcome

**Forwardable blurb:**
> [Your company] is [one-line description]. We're [traction]. Given [Name]'s focus on [thesis area], I'd love to connect for feedback on [specific question].

### Follow-Ups

- **No response after 5 days:** Brief bump with new information
- **No response after 10 days:** Final check-in, offer specific time slots
- **After a call:** Thank-you email with agreed next steps within 24 hours
- **After a pass:** Gracious thank-you, ask for feedback, stay in touch

### Investor Updates

Monthly or quarterly updates to existing investors and prospects:
- Metrics (revenue, users, growth rate)
- Wins (customers, partnerships, press)
- Challenges and how you're addressing them
- Ask (intros, hires, feedback)
- Upcoming milestones

Keep it concise — 5-7 bullet points max. Investors scan; they don't read.

## Quality Standards

- **Specificity over hype.** "50% MoM growth" beats "rapidly growing."
- **Source everything.** Market data needs a citation.
- **One claim per slide.** Don't crowd.
- **Know your audience.** Seed investors care about team and market; Series A cares about traction and unit economics.
- **Honest about risks.** Acknowledging weaknesses builds credibility.

## Pitfalls

- **TAM inflation.** Using the entire industry size without segmenting destroys credibility.
- **Generic outreach.** Investors can smell template emails.
- **Deck overload.** >15 slides means you haven't edited enough.
- **No ask.** Always end with a specific, reasonable next step.
- **Following up too aggressively.** 3 touches max over 2 weeks.
- **Neglecting warm paths.** A warm intro is 10x more effective than cold.
