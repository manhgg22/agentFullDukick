# Fundraising Communications

Class-level skill for all investor-facing work: creating materials and drafting outreach. These two surfaces share the same constraints — they must be concise, internally consistent, and calibrated to the investor's time.

## When to use

- Building or updating a pitch deck, one-pager, investor memo, or accelerator application
- Creating financial projections, use-of-funds tables, or milestone plans
- Drafting cold emails, warm intro blurbs, follow-ups, or investor update emails
- Any task where the audience is an angel, VC, strategic investor, or accelerator

## Part 1: Investor Materials

### Principles
- One idea per slide. Dense decks signal uncertainty.
- Every number needs a source or a conservative assumption stated inline.
- Keep materials internally consistent across all fundraising assets.

### Common deliverables
- **Pitch deck** — 10-15 slides covering problem, solution, market, traction, team, ask
- **One-pager** — single-page summary for quick sharing
- **Investor memo** — detailed narrative for serious conversations
- **Accelerator application** — answers tailored to the specific program's criteria
- **Financial model** — projections with clear assumptions and sensitivity analysis
- **Use-of-funds table** — where the money goes, by category and timeline
- **Milestone plan** — what you will accomplish with this round

### Quality gates
- Does every slide answer "so what?" within 3 seconds?
- Are numbers consistent between the deck, memo, and spreadsheet?
- Is the ask specific and justified?
- Can a busy investor understand the core argument in 60 seconds?

## Part 2: Investor Outreach

### Principles
- Every message must earn the recipient's attention in the first line.
- Warm intros beat cold emails; cold emails beat no outreach.
- Follow up once, then move on. Persistence is not pestering if spaced correctly.
- Update emails are relationship maintenance, not sales pitches.

### Message types
- **Cold email** — brief, specific ask, clear value proposition, soft CTA
- **Warm intro blurb** — one paragraph the connector can forward verbatim
- **Follow-up** — reference the original thread, add one new piece of information
- **Update email** — milestones, metrics, asks (if any), gratitude

### Banned patterns
- "Excited to share"
- "Revolutionary" or "disruptive" without evidence
- Vague asks: "we'd love to chat"
- Mass-customized emails that feel templated
- Follow-ups within 48 hours

## Pitfalls

- **Over-building the deck before validating the narrative.** Story first, slides second.
- **Inconsistent numbers.** One typo in the financial model destroys credibility.
- **Generic outreach.** Investors receive hundreds of pitches; specificity is the only filter.
- **Update emails that are only good news.** Investors trust you more when you share setbacks and how you handled them.
